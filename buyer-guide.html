Statutory Progression

The 5 Steps of Purchasing in California

Follow these sequential requirements before you head to your local California licensed firearms dealer (FFL).

1
Step 1: Primary Identity

California Driver's License & The "Federal Limits" Trap

CA Penal Code § 26845

Under California law, you must present a valid, unexpired government photo identification issued by the California Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV).

Critical Trap: Does your CA Driver's License say "FEDERAL LIMITS APPLY"?

Look at the top right corner of your California Driver's License or ID card. If it displays "FEDERAL LIMITS APPLY" (non-REAL ID), the California DOJ will immediately reject your application unless you also provide certified secondary proof of legal presence.

If your license says "Federal Limits Apply", you MUST bring ONE of the following:

  • Valid, unexpired United States Passport or U.S. Passport Card
  • Original or certified copy of a U.S. Birth Certificate
  • Valid, unexpired Permanent Resident Card (Green Card)
  • Certificate of Naturalization or U.S. Citizenship

2
Step 2: Handgun Mandatory Proof

Proof of California Residency (Handguns Only)

CA Penal Code § 26845 & 11 CCR § 4045.1

If purchasing a handgun, California state law strictly mandates that the buyer provide a second document verifying their legal California residence. The name and residential address on this document must match the address on your driver's license exactly.

ACCEPTED Documents (Must Be Current)

  • ✓ Current DMV Vehicle Registration: The most common, fool-proof document. Must bear your current address.
  • ✓ Municipal Utility Bill: From a government municipality (City Water, Trash, SMUD, LADWP, PG&E). Must be within the last 90 days.
  • ✓ Residential Lease Agreement: Formally signed and dated by both tenant and landlord/property manager.
  • ✓ Government-Issued Property Deed: Official property tax bill or county deed.

STRICTLY REJECTED by CA DOJ

  • ✗ Cell Phone Bills: (Verizon, T-Mobile, AT&T) — strictly prohibited by statute.
  • ✗ Credit Card or Bank Statements: Even if stamped by the bank.
  • ✗ Cable TV / Home Internet Bills: (Comcast, Spectrum) are private utility bills and generally rejected.
  • ✗ Pay Stubs / W-2s: Not recognized as residency documents under CA CCR § 4045.1.
Address Mismatch Note: If you recently moved and your driver's license displays your old address, you must update your address in the DMV system and bring a current DMV printout or valid vehicle registration showing your updated home.
3
Step 3: State Safety Exam

Acquire a Firearm Safety Certificate (FSC)

CA Penal Code § 31615

Unless you hold a qualifying California statutory exemption (such as an active peace officer, valid CCW permit holder, or active military with ID), you must possess a valid California Firearm Safety Certificate (FSC) before purchasing any firearm.

4
Step 4: Background Screening

DROS Application & The 10-Day Waiting Period

CA Penal Code § 26815 & § 28220

Once you select your firearm and verify your documents, the dealer will electronically submit your Dealer's Record of Sale (DROS) application to the California Department of Justice Bureau of Firearms.

Exact 240-Hour Mandatory Wait (10 Full Days): California law mandates a mandatory waiting period of ten 24-hour periods down to the exact second from the timestamp on your DROS paperwork. If your DROS is submitted on June 1st at 2:15 PM, the dealer cannot legally release the firearm to you until June 11th at 2:15:01 PM.
The 30-Day Expiration Window: You must pick up your firearm within 30 calendar days of submitting the DROS. If you fail to pick it up by Day 30, the DROS cancels by operation of law, and you must restart the entire background check and waiting period process from scratch.

*Note: The California state DROS background check fee is standard across the state (approximately $37.19), collected by the dealer on Day 1.

5
Step 5: Delivery & Safe Transport

Safe Handling Demonstration & Transport Home

CA Penal Code § 26850 & § 25610

When your 10-day waiting period has elapsed and your background check is approved, you will return to the shop for final delivery. Two final requirements must be completed:

The Safe Handling Demonstration (SHD)

In the presence of the certified DOJ instructor, you must personally perform a physical safe handling demonstration using inert dummy rounds:

  • Check that the firearm is clear and unloaded
  • Lock the action open and visually inspect the chamber
  • Load a dummy round into the magazine/cylinder
  • Chamber and safely eject the dummy round
  • Apply the firearm safety device (cable lock)

Firearm Safety Device (FSD)

California mandates that every firearm leaving a dealer must be accompanied by an approved California DOJ-certified lock or safe affidavit (PC § 23635):

  • Most new firearms come from the manufacturer with a certified cable lock inside the box.
  • If the lock is absent, you must purchase a DOJ-certified lock (~$10) or sign an approved Safe Affidavit verifying you own an approved gun safe at home.

Legal Transport to Your Home (PC § 25610)

When leaving the gun store, your firearm must be completely unloaded and secured inside the trunk of your vehicle or locked inside a fully locked container (other than the glove compartment or center console). Ammunition should be kept separate.

Clarity & Compliance

First-Time Buyer FAQs

Can non-U.S. citizens purchase a firearm in California?

Yes. Lawful Permanent Residents (Green Card holders) can legally purchase firearms in California by presenting their valid CA driver's license, proof of residency, and their unexpired Permanent Resident Card (Form I-551). Non-immigrant visa holders generally face strict federal prohibitions unless qualifying under narrow exceptions (such as possessing a valid California hunting license).

Can I purchase ammunition on the same day as my firearm?

Under California law, you can purchase ammunition through the licensed dealer, but it requires an electronic DOJ background check ($1 standard check if you have an active firearm record in the state system, or $19 basic check). Many buyers purchase target ammunition on the day they pick up their firearm at the conclusion of the 10-day waiting period.

What happens if I make a typo on the 4473 / DROS form?

Dealers are required by law to have you carefully review every line of the DROS summary before submitting. If a name or address is misspelled, it will cause a DROS rejection or delay from the state DOJ. Take your time at the counter to verify that your street name, apartment number, and spelling match your documents letter-for-letter.
